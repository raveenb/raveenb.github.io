# The Synthesis of Popper and Hegel

### 1. The Inductive Stack
In this model, learning begins as a series of **Inductive Pillars**. These are statistical generalizations similar to those found in current Transformer models. However, instead of being "flat" predictions, they are modeled as stacks within a high-rank tensor.

### 2. The Popperian Filter
According to Karl Popper, we cannot prove a theory is true; we can only prove it has not yet been falsified. In the PKT framework:
- Deductive reasoning acts as a **falsification engine**.
- It "prunes" the inductive pillars from the top down.
- Any pillar that violates a core logical axiom or physical law is collapsed.

### 3. The Hegelian Episteme
We exist in an "episteme" where many provisional truths agree. As our Knowledge Tensor $\mathcal{T}$ grows, we see a convergence of patterns. However, we remain aware that this convergence is provisional—valid only until a new deductive rule or "black swan" observation necessitates a reconfiguration of the tensor.
